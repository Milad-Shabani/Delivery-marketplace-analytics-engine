Sources & methodology

Everything on this page is computed from the synthetic dataset in this repository. No real company data is used.

ComponentApproach
Data generationReproducible NumPy/pandas simulation (fixed seed) of ~12,000 customers, 320 restaurants, 520 riders and ~167k orders over 17 months, with seasonality, a growth trend, churn/retention curves and realistic financial relationships. v2 adds two tables: observed marketing spend by month × channel, and rider online/busy hours.
KPI layer34 scorecard KPIs computed from the order table in src/analytics/kpis.py. Delivered orders = Delivered + Refunded. Contribution profit is before promotions (the model’s headline definition); “after promotions” subtracts promotion cost. The scorecard compares the latest month with the previous month.
ForecastingSeasonal-naive, moving-average and Holt-Winters models compared via rolling-origin (temporal) validation; the best model is selected per metric by WAPE.
OptimizationSciPy trust-constr solves a concave-response promotion-budget allocation across city × segment cells, maximizing incremental contribution profit under a fixed budget.
SegmentationRule-based on realized recency / frequency / monetary value and subscription status — not random labels.
KPI windowsCustomer sign-ups are generated over a fixed window that ends on 2026-08-23, so the final month holds only part of its sign-ups. Sign-up-driven KPIs (new customers, new-customer share, marketing spend, CAC, marketing % of revenue) therefore use a trailing 3-month window, tagged “3M”.
LimitationsAll data is synthetic: patterns such as retention, channel economics and city margins are model outputs, not market evidence. Per-order unit economics use the last 3 months; LTV is the observed lifetime profit to date, not a projection. Marketing spend and rider hours are generated tables too, with built-in CAC inflation for paid channels, so the gap between “observed” and planning CAC is a modelled outcome, not market evidence.
